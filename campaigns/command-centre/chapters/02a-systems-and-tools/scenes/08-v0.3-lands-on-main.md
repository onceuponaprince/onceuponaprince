# Scene 2a-08 — v0.3 lands on main

*Chapter 2a — Systems and tools · Campaign: [[command-centre]]*

*For two days v0.3 of the SPORE stack existed as a scatter of parallel worktree branches and merged PRs. This scene is the hour it stopped being branches and became one main anyone could trust, and the discovery that the consolidation, not the building, is where the system first became real.*

---

## Set the Stage

### How did we get here?

Scene 06 installed the handoff-board pattern: parallel agents, isolated git worktrees, contract-freeze before dispatch. Scene 07 turned the browser-bot into a product instrument and surfaced the bug the product is meant to make legible. Between and around those two scenes, a large v0.3 build accumulated. Not in one place. Across `feat/v0.3-remaining-build`, five `impl/w-*` worktrees (cse-engine, graph-default, mcp-sse, openrouter, wasm-wire), a v0.2.0-alpha.1 release-prep line, an OSS public-release pass, and roughly forty `feat(spore-*)` commits numbered v0.3 #1 through #13. The work was real. It was also nowhere in particular: branches and PRs, not a main.

### Where are we going?

One consolidated `origin/main` that carries the whole of v0.3, with the test gate green and the parallel streams collapsed into a single truthful trunk. The destination is not "more shipped". It is "one place that is true".

### State of the world (project context)

The `borai` monorepo. The SPORE Rust workspace, roughly two dozen crates, plus the `ops/borai-cc/` fanout infrastructure (browser-bot, router, cli-runner). Eight worktrees off one repo. Local `main` sitting twelve commits behind `origin/main` before the consolidation began. PRs #26 (local-model-triage), #28 (contextops/spore-hygiene), and #32 (v0.3-remaining-build) already merged upstream; the worktree branches not yet integrated.

### State of the hero ([[solo-thesis-holder]])

The solo founder watching this build in public. The want is throughput: more worktrees, more parallel agents, more commits per day. The need is a trunk they can stand on without checking. The dominant objection is the one the whole consensus carries: *isn't more parallelism just more speed?*

### State of the protagonist ([[prince]])

Mid-debug, not mid-celebration. The consolidation was supposed to be housekeeping at the end of a research session. It became the place the session's worst bug actually lived. The mood is the specific one of finding out the cleanup step was the real work.

### This moment in relation to goals

Chapter 2a installs the infrastructure the webapp will stand on. This is the scene where that infrastructure stops being a set of parallel experiments and becomes load-bearing: everything v0.3 on one main, tested, pushed. The scaffolding earns its name here or it does not earn it at all.

### Why now?

The parallel build had reached the point where unintegrated work stopped being progress and started being liability. Five worktrees and a remaining-build branch, all v0.3, all diverging from the same base, are not an asset until they flow into one place. Scene 07's fanout work was the forcing function: it could not be validated against a trunk that did not yet exist.

## Progress the moment

### Goal for this session

- Consolidate every v0.3 worktree branch (excluding `worktree-inbox-schema-writer`) onto local `main`, gate it on the full test suite, reconcile with `origin/main`, push. Remove the spent worktrees and branches. Leave no broken intermediate state.

### Moment-by-moment capture

- The pre-48h trunk already held the bulk of v0.3: spore-orchestra LocalModelTriager (#1), spore-router adaptive tool ranking (#3) and capability-gating (#9) and in-process borai-graph (#6), spore-tools MCP SSE (#5), spore-session portable cross-session (#10), spore-plugins WASM loader (#12), spore-providers coercion (#8), spore-cse deterministic classifier (#7), spore-sandbox flip (#11), the ContextOps hygiene gateway, `team_fanout` multi-provider browser. Plus v0.2.0-alpha.1 release prep, DoD and gated-e2e records, OSS v1.0 public-release engineering, and the v0.3 #6-13 execution spec.
- Session start: `main` was carrying ~43 modified + 8 untracked files. Two distinct bodies of work entangled in one dirty tree: this session's `ops/borai-cc` fanout infra fixes, and an unrelated v0.3 SPORE WIP (37 files, a new `spore-patterns` crate). Decision: separate them. Infra fixes committed alone (`21fe7e9`). SPORE WIP committed as an explicit snapshot (`9d2ef4d`), generated `output/` and runtime `.claire/` deliberately excluded.
- Conflict prediction before any merge (`git merge-tree`, non-destructive): four of six branches clean, two conflicting on a single file (`spore-cse/src/handlers.rs`).
- Merged in order: graph-default, mcp-sse, openrouter, wasm-wire clean. cse-engine: one trivial import-union conflict, resolved. feat/v0.3-remaining-build: clean under `ort` (it was itself already a consolidation of the impl/w-* set, which is why the chain was low-conflict).
- Test gate on the merged result: `cargo test --workspace` 409 passed, 1 ignored, 0 failed, 0 compile errors. `pytest` caught exactly one failure: this session's own atomic-input fix breaking a `FakePage` test double. Fixed the double properly (`c86498a`), 44 passed.
- Reconciled with `origin/main` (predicted clean, merged clean), pushed. Worktrees removed (six), merged branches deleted (six), `worktree-inbox-schema-writer` preserved. Remote `feat/v0.3-remaining-build` pruned, root `output/` and `.claire/` gitignored (`b93c5e9`). `origin/main` and local `main`: 0/0.

### What's changing?

The understanding shifted mid-scene. The consolidation was scoped as cleanup. It was where the session's most expensive bug (the silent prompt-truncation in Scene 07) actually became visible, because the bug only had somewhere to be caught once there was a trunk and a gate to catch it against. Parallel branches were motion. The merge plus the gate was the first moment any of it was true. That reframing is the scene, and it is the essay.
